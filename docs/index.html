Timberborn co-op mod · v1.4.0-beta19 · beta

One map.
A colony each.

Play Timberborn with friends without sharing a colony. Each player runs their own districts, beavers, stock and science on one shared map, with no borders, and the colonies' roads meet only at trading posts, where they barter. Co-op, not a race: nobody wins, and everyone can help.

New in 1.4.0-beta19

A waiting room for new games and saves, your colony's controls only, and no borders

beta19 brings the waiting room to hosting a save from the main menu. beta18 added it to the New Game screens: invite your friends, everyone marks themselves ready, and you all start the new world together, with a colony each from the first second. beta17 greys out another colony's district's migration controls. beta16 lets a Trading Post be placed before its roads (it trades once two colonies' roads reach it), keeps other mods' building settings to each building's owner, draws each colony's roads far more clearly with Ctrl+L, and makes the trading posts window movable. beta15 removed colony land: no borders, no outlines and no distance to keep when founding. Build, plant and cut anywhere; the one rule left is that two colonies' roads never join, except through a Trading Post, which now goes between two road ends. The rules are in TWO-COLONIES.md. beta14 shares dev mode's Add 1000 Science with every player; it desynced the first beta12 session. beta13 rewrote the tooltips of the two colony settings so they can't be mixed up. beta12 went over everything that keeps the players' games in step, and fixed what it found: Wonders on the tick, deletions the same for everyone, players who follow a host that slows down, and a direct player whose connection dies no longer freezing the rest. The details are in the beta12 release notes, the review report and the changelog.

Invite first, start together

beta18 and beta19. On the New Game difficulty page choose Host co-op game beside Start and name your settlement; for a save, Load Game → Host co-op game in the main menu. Friends join the Co-op Game page by Steam invite or IP and press I'm ready; you press Start Game. Everyone loads the same world at once, paused, and a friend without a colony can place their district center straight away.

No borders

beta15. Colonies no longer have land. Build, plant, cut and found anywhere, right up to another colony's buildings and roads, as long as your roads don't join theirs: keep your paths a cell away from their roads and district center and off the cell at their buildings' doors, and don't put a building's entrance on or beside their road. Ctrl+L shows each colony's roads in its color.

Place the post, then the roads

beta16. A Trading Post is the one place two colonies' roads meet: one colony's road at each half's door. Place it wherever the colonies will meet, before the roads or after; it trades once both roads reach it, and its panel says Not trading yet until then. Exchanges of goods, science and beavers worked in a beta15 session.

Your warehouse, your settings

beta16. Settings other mods add to a building, like MixedStorage's warehouse and pile goods, follow the same rule as this mod's own: only the building's colony changes them. Another player's try is refused with a notice.

Clearer roads, a window that moves

beta16. Ctrl+L now draws every road cell as a bright square in its colony's color, easy to read by day or night. Drag the trading posts window (Ctrl+T) by its title; it opens where you left it. The All Posts button no longer covers the panel's scroll bar.

Your districts, your migration

beta17. In the Migration tab (F7), another colony's district shows its automatic migration settings greyed out, as they are, and the manual migration buttons between it and yours are greyed out too. Changes there were always refused; now they can't be clicked at all.

Barely played

712 automated checks cover all of it (StabilityTests 373 and RuntimeChecks 339). beta16, beta17 and the waiting room (beta18 and beta19) have not been played yet. beta12, beta14 and beta15 each had one short session: a founding and building stayed in step, beta12's one desync (dev mode's science) did not come back, and beta15's trading posts traded goods and beavers. The test scripts' Script D (the waiting room) and lines B8aa to B8af are the ones to try first, then B9a, B8t to B8z and C1b.

A game, start to finish

Your own colony in four steps

Any save works: a new game, an old colony, or a BeaverBuddies multi-start map. The host's colony is whatever is already there; everyone else founds theirs.

  1. Everyone installs the mod

    The same zip, then restart. Players on different builds cannot join each other, and the mod checks its own files too. Install guide

  2. The host invites, everyone readies up

    A new game: on the New Game difficulty page choose Host co-op game and name the settlement. A save: Load Game → Host co-op game. Either way the waiting room opens: Invite Friends (or share your IP), friends press I'm ready, you press Start Game, and everyone loads together.

  3. Found your colony

    A message offers you a district center: at once after a waiting room, once the host unpauses in a hosted save. Place it anywhere its roads won't join another colony's: it appears already built, with beavers, food and water.

  4. Build, grow, trade

    Build anywhere, right up to your neighbors, but keep your roads apart from theirs. To trade, each of you builds a road up to one spot; either then puts a Trading Post between the two ends and makes an offer.

What you get

Everything the original BeaverBuddies and the Stability Fork do, plus a colony of your own.

Your colony, yours alone

Your districts, beavers, stock, working hours and marks. Nobody can pause, rename, demolish or re-staff your buildings, and your beavers build, cut, plant and gather only for you.

Build anywhere

No land and no borders: build and mark wherever you like, right up to another colony's buildings. The one rule is that two colonies' roads never join, except through a Trading Post. Each colony's roads show as bright squares in its color while you place a building, or any time with Ctrl+L.

Trading posts

The one place colonies' roads meet. Offer goods, science or beavers, or a gift; the other player accepts; the beavers do the carrying. Say what you are looking for, keep a reserve on a standing deal, offer the last exchange again in one click. How an exchange works

Your own science

If the host chooses, each colony earns its own science and unlocks its own buildings and bot types. Your inventors work for you; your toolbar is yours.

Colonies are remembered

The save knows you by your Steam ID (without Steam, by an id kept on your computer): the same colony every time, whoever hosts. Away for the evening? Ask a friend to look after your colony. A colony whose player stops coming, or that dies out, is handed to a neighbor (everyone is warned the day before), and its player can found again.

The Stability Fork underneath

Steam friend invites, a connection panel with chat and a speed boost, teammates' cursors and selections, pings, and the fixes for the crashes and desyncs that made co-op painful. Turn Separate colonies for new games off and a game is the Stability Fork's shared co-op, with none of the colony model running.

Trading posts

Barter, not a market: play with the real panel

No prices and no currency. An exchange is "this many of one thing for that many of another, so many times", and nothing is given before what it was exchanged for is in. This is a working copy of the Trading Post's panel, drawn like the game, with the mod's own rules: make an offer, watch each colony's workers bring its side to its own half, and see the round cross only when both are in.

Things to try

  1. Press Make offer. Sarah accepts after a moment; your workers bring the logs to your half and hers bring the gears to hers. The round crosses only when both bars are full, and the ledger gets a line.
  2. Untick answers by itself, make an offer, then switch to Sarah: you see the offer from her side, with Accept and Decline. Every exchange in the game is agreed this way, one player per side. (The panel names a colony by the player who runs it, as the game does.)
  3. Set Workers on your half to 0 during a round: your side stops arriving and the status line says why. Nothing crosses until it is in.
  4. Press Cancel exchange mid-round. The other player must agree; until then nothing moves. Once it ends, what waited on each half goes back home.
  5. Type 150 in an amount box: the line turns red and Make offer grays out. Set Rounds higher instead, or tick Repeat until cancelled.

Under the hood

Two colonies, one truth

Timberborn is a single-player game, so co-op keeps every player's copy identical instead of streaming one game to everyone. MultiColony adds rules to that, and checks them.

The host decides

Every action goes through the host, which seats players, writes the actor's colony into the action, judges it against the rules, and plays and forwards it, or refuses it with a reason. Guests never judge, so the computers can't disagree.

Ownership is saved

Who owns each district, building and mark is in the save, the same on every computer. Beavers choose their work from these only.

A desync is caught the tick it happens

Every colony state change folds into a digest the host sends with each heartbeat; a guest that differs stops at once, with both numbers in the log. A full colony check follows once a day.

The full rules, with what still reaches across colonies (water, weather, area effects) and every known limit: TWO-COLONIES.md.

Good to know before you play

  • Everyone must run the exact same build of the mod and the same version of the game; the join is refused otherwise. Other mods should match too (you get a warning).
  • Join before the host starts or builds. Nobody can join after the first tick, or after the host places or marks anything while waiting paused; the host is asked before that first change, and a guest's change while the host waits is refused. A waiting room (a new game, or a save hosted from the main menu) closes at Start Game: join it from the main menu, before then.
  • Only one BeaverBuddies at a time. If another is enabled, the main menu names it and this one stays out of the way.
  • The map is shared. Water, droughts, badwater tides and weather reach everyone; a dam upstream still changes what flows downstream. The game ends only when every beaver on the map is gone.
  • Text is English only for what this mod adds; other languages show the English text there.
